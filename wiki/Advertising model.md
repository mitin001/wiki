The advertising model — the compromise the Internet economy settled on between making information and services hosted on it available for free while at the same time subjecting its stewards to the pressures of the marketplace, to disastrous consequences. Social media is free to use but it modifies your behavior to suit the moneyed interests. The advertising model is the opposite of the subscription model, in which the user pays for the service directly, reducing the incentives for companies to seek the income stream from roundabout schemes.

## The compromise

> Everything on the internet must be purely public, must be available for free. ... But at the same time, we also believed, with equal fervor, in this other thing that was completely incompatible, which is we loved our tech entrepreneurs. ... How do you celebrate entrepreneurship when everything's free?

[Jaron Lanier].

[Jaron Lanier]: https://www.ted.com/talks/jaron_lanier_how_we_need_to_remake_the_internet

## The most effective ads are the ones near the negative stimuli

Because the users weren't paying for the service, Facebook and other social media companies made the money by offering their enterprise customers what they wanted: A/B testing. Social media treated their users as lab rats. The large corporations, Facebook's enterprise customers, were the experimenters. They quickly found that pitting users against each other maximized their engagement with their ads.

> Whether positive stimuli are more effective than negative ones in different circumstances, the negative ones are cheaper. They're the bargain stimuli. So what I mean by that is it's much easier to lose trust than to build trust. It takes a long time to build love. It takes a short time to ruin love. Now the customers of these behavior modification empires are on a very fast loop. They're almost like high-frequency traders. They're getting feedbacks from their spends or whatever their activities are if they're not spending, and they see what's working, and then they do more of that. And so they're getting the quick feedback, which means they're responding more to the negative emotions, because those are the ones that rise faster.

[Jaron Lanier].

## The subscription model

HBO and Netflix ushered in the age of peak TV. Jaron Lanier attributes this to the subscription model. Contrast that to the TV we got with ads.

[Jaron Lanier].

Similarly, if we start paying for social media, we will get peak social media.

## Always choose the subscription model over the advertising model

If you want to use a service, go with the option for which you can pay directly.

> We cannot have a society in which, if two people wish to communicate, the only way that can happen is if it's financed by a third person who wishes to manipulate them.

[Jaron Lanier].
